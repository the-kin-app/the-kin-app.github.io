01 — the quiet part

A million people within walking distance. Nobody to talk to.

Cities put us metres from each other and screens keep us hours apart. Social platforms were tuned for time on the glass — global, para-social, endless — and the awkwardness of a first sentence kills almost every real moment before it starts.

Loneliness is now a public health problem with a very friendly interface.

The internet is full. The street is empty. The most valuable thing a person can do is a physical thing — and almost nothing online is built to cause one.

Then the dot leaves the logo, and starts paying attention.

02 — meet Min

Not a feed.
A companion.

You just talk. Min picks up your interests, your taste, your rhythm — no questionnaire, no profile to maintain. Then it watches the block around you for the one thing you'd actually want to know.

What Min learns stays on your phone. Nothing about you ever crosses the wire in one piece, and there is no dossier to browse — not even for you. That's the point.

04 — the four nots

We copied nothing from the companies that got us here.

Privacy, not profit at all costs

We don't sell data — not as a policy, as an architecture. Every consumer of your persona has a declared allow-list, and zero weight means zero egress.

Local, not global

Nothing from the other side of the planet. Kin only knows the few hundred metres around you, because that's the only place you can walk to.

Social, not para-social

No followers, no audience, no performing. Kin's only successful outcome is two people in the same room, talking.

Informative, not addictive

No engagement ranking, no streaks, no manufactured urgency. Proactive contact is capped by design — the app is not allowed to nag you.

the whole idea

The only app that wins when you put it down.

We're building it in Helsinki first, one neighbourhood at a time, because a hyperlocal network only works once it's dense. Get in early and you help decide what your street feels like.
