# Min — updated potential revenue at scale

14 September 2026. Illustrative steady-state scenarios, not forecasts, market-size estimates or proposed changes to the canonical business plan.

## Scope and accounting

This model combines community licences, consumer membership and separately monetised activity bookings. All scale inputs are full-year averages: contracted communities, average monthly active users, paying membership share and bookings. A year-end run rate must not be presented as revenue earned during a ramp year.

Net receipts are community fees ex-VAT, consumer proceeds after assumed VAT/store fees, and retained activity fees after tax/payment processing. They are comparable cash-economics measures, not a formal accounting revenue recognition policy. Activity revenue is transactional, not ARR. Membership is cancellable; its annualised receipts are not contracted backlog.

Sponsored and independent consumer MAU are mutually exclusive each month. A community is a contracted local cluster/portfolio with approximately 2,000 eligible residents, not necessarily one building or one parent customer. Each supplies 500 MAU, deduplicated across contracts. Parent operators may buy multiple clusters, but portfolio discounts must be reflected in realised contract price.

Sponsored users do not also buy the same consumer membership. Newcomer packages and café visit fees are excluded. Activity fees apply only to separate, completed paid bookings not included in any membership/contract/package. The €4 fee is assumed supplier-funded, not an extra user charge for already included coordination; this supplier willingness to pay is unproven. Each booking is one attendee booking, not a whole party. No activity GMV is counted as Min revenue.

## Target unit assumptions

- Contract: €20,000 annually for 2,000 eligible residents / 500 average sponsored MAU.
- Membership: 6% of independent consumer MAU pay €19.99/month. Annual proceeds per continuously paying equivalent: €19.99 / 1.255 × 80% × 12 = €152.91. The 25.5% VAT and 20% blended store fee are modelling conventions carried over from the previous review, not a prediction of international tax/channel mix.
- Paid activity: 0.15 completed, separately monetised attendee bookings per total MAU/month, €4 retained per booking. Thus €7.20 annual activity receipts per MAU. Frequency is averaged over all MAU, including non-bookers.
- Revenue does not increase automatically with geographic density. Communities and consumers are explicit volumes.

## Illustrative operating costs

- Shared usage-linked infrastructure, inference and basic support: €0.40 per total MAU/month.
- Account delivery/support: €2,000 per contracted community/year, additional to shared usage costs.
- Booking exception/support/refund reserve: €0.75 per booking, additional to payment costs already removed from the €4 receipt. Activity providers deliver the underlying activity; Min has no host, meal or admission subsidy in this model.
- Community maintenance acquisition: 12% annual contract loss × €12,000 fully loaded replacement contract CAC. Includes initial onboarding/acquisition work; recurring account delivery is separate.
- Independent audience maintenance: 8% monthly active-user loss × €12 per replacement active × 12. Blended CAC includes organic and paid acquisition delivery cost. Sponsored audience replenishment is assumed covered by the partner's normal distribution and the account service budget, rather than free. If it requires additional Min spending, add it.
- Annual fixed central and local overhead: €600k early rollout; €1.5m regional; €5m multi-market; €8m international; €20m large network; €35m global network. These are explicit budget allowances for product, engineering, management, compliance, local operations and overhead. Acquisition costs above are fully loaded and must not be charged again in overhead.
- Surplus excludes tax, financing, working-capital changes and net expansion spending. This is not a funding/runway forecast. New-user and new-community acquisition beyond replacement must be funded separately.

## One-million-MAU sensitivity

All cases hold the same 1,000 communities, 500k sponsored MAU, 500k independent MAU, audience maintenance assumptions and €8m fixed overhead. Delivery changes with booking count. This isolates commercial performance, not every correlated downside.

| Input/result | Downside | Target | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Annual community contract | €12,000 | €20,000 | €24,000 |
| Independent consumer paid share | 3% | 6% | 8% |
| Monthly paid bookings/MAU | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.25 |
| Annual net receipts | €16.69m | €31.79m | €42.12m |
| Delivery costs | €7.25m | €8.15m | €9.05m |
| Maintenance acquisition | €7.20m | €7.20m | €7.20m |
| Fixed overhead | €8.00m | €8.00m | €8.00m |
| Operating surplus | −€5.76m | €8.44m | €17.87m |

The target's €31.79m comprises €24.59m annual community/membership receipts and €7.20m transactional receipts. It requires 30,000 average paying independent members and 1.8m annual attendee bookings. This is 150,000 monthly bookings — 5,000/day on a 30-day-month convention.

At target one-million-user scale, roughly 63% of receipts are community contracts. That is a material business-model change, not simply improved café monetisation. Without activity revenue and its associated support expense, target receipts are €24.59m and operating surplus €2.59m; the contract/membership business can stand on its own under these assumptions.

A 50:50 sponsored/consumer mix at the target unit assumptions reaches €100m receipts at approximately 3.15m MAU, requiring about 3,146 contracted communities with 6.29m eligible sponsored residents and approximately 94,400 average independent paying members. This is an implied scale requirement, not evidence that the audience or customers exist.

## Expansion and capital

A plateau can generate a surplus while a fast-growing company still burns cash. At the target one-million-user mix, adding 100,000 net active users would mean about 100 additional communities plus 50,000 independent actives. At the assumed acquisition costs, that is €1.2m + €0.6m = €1.8m initial acquisition spending before incremental fixed capacity, ramp losses and working capital. Actual throughput, timing and attribution of acquisition budgets require a cohort cash-flow forecast.

Community gross receipts of €20k are not validated by incumbent pricing: the contracts must be sold. The 6% conversion at €19.99 is more demanding than the earlier cheap subscription proposition. The activity line requires booking integrations and operations absent from the current one-to-one MVP. The model only applies once the revised capabilities and commercial delivery exist.

## Recommended use

Use the €14m–€32m ladder as a concrete description of a successful emerging company, and the €95m–€191m ladder as a venture upside. Do not present any as a dated base-case forecast. First measure three paid community pilots, six-month ongoing participation, real subscription purchases, and renewal/portfolio expansion intent backed by contracts.
